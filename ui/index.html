Built on what learning research says works, to get the most out of the paper in the least time: the big picture and the key terms first, then short segments on the method, results and discussion, each opening with a question to keep in mind and ending with its answer; every figure explained, the limitations read critically, and recall questions at the end, answered after a pause. The description lists the questions again, to test yourself the next day. Related work and the appendix are left out. About 12 to 14 min for a 20-page paper.
The methodology, results and discussion in detail, in the paper's order: how the study or system works step by step, every key result with its figures and tables, then what the results mean and their limitations. The introduction and related work only feed a short opening; the appendix is left out. Often 15 to 20 min.
Each section gets the time its content needs: its key numbers, method steps and claims, and a walk through each figure and table. Nothing is padded to reach a length or cut to fit one. Related work and the appendix are left out.
Every sentence of the paper, from the title to the last section, explained in plain words while the highlighter sits on that exact sentence. Headings are read as they are, every author by name, and each figure and table caption is explained with the figure on screen. Long: about 5 minutes of video per page, and recording it in your cloned voice takes several hours.
After S. Keshav, "How to Read a Paper" (2007). From a finished job you can make the next pass without the model reading the paper again.
Cloning is slow on a laptop (~1 min per sentence). Only clone a voice you have permission to use.
Questions are written from the paper and every number in them is checked. A longer set is saved as quiz.md for testing students.
Optional. Every finished video gets its own numbered folder here, in the order you made the videos, with the captions, description, script and narration inside. The job folder keeps its own copy either way.
Everything runs on this computer: Ollama, poppler, ffmpeg and the voice model. No internet needed.
